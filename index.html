This Week's Story — Consumer Deep-Dive

The consumer sector is Dubai's standout success story. Domestic card spending has surpassed pre-conflict levels at 105%, international card spend is even higher at 106%, and mall footfall has recovered to 94% — essentially normal.

The real headline is remittances: outbound transfers surged to 167% of baseline (a +984% week-on-week jump), likely reflecting pent-up cross-border payment demand finally clearing. Inbound remittances hit exactly 100% — a clean return to normal.

Dubai residents are spending, shopping, and transferring money as if the conflict never happened.

Key Insight
Dubai's consumer economy has effectively returned to pre-conflict normal. The challenge is no longer demand — it's ensuring supply chains (trade, logistics) and capital markets (investor confidence) can keep pace.

📋 Methodology & Data Sources ▼

Baseline Period

Pre-conflict weekly averages calculated from the 12-month period prior to the onset of hostilities. Each KPI's baseline represents its "normal" operating level.

Recovery Percentage

Current week's value divided by the baseline value, expressed as a percentage. 100% = full recovery to pre-conflict normal.

Status Thresholds

Critical (<50%): Severely below normal — active disruption or collapse
Recovering (50–80%): Improving but materially below normal
Normal (80–100%): Approaching or at pre-conflict levels
Above Baseline (>100%): Exceeding pre-conflict levels

Week-on-Week (WoW)

Percentage change from the previous reporting week.

Data Sources

Dubai Economy & Tourism sectoral reporting, DXB airport operations, Jebel Ali port authority, Dubai Financial Market, UAE Central Bank remittance data, Network International card transaction data, Dubai Land Department, DTCM hotel occupancy reporting.

Frequency

Weekly, published every Wednesday. Data reflects the preceding 7-day period (Thursday–Wednesday).
